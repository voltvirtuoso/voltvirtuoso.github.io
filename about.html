A short introduction

Electrical Engineering training, hands-on embedded work, systems operations, and a continuing interest in the bridge between physical equipment and useful software.

I’m Haroon Raza, an Instrument Engineer with a Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering from COMSATS University Islamabad. My work has taken me across asset verification, embedded automation, VoIP infrastructure, firmware simulation, computer vision prototypes, and technical team operations.

I enjoy projects where hardware has a clear job to do: measure a condition, make a safe choice, report what happened, and remain manageable for the person who uses it. That is why I keep returning to embedded systems, control logic, IoT, and instrumentation.

I'm a curious learner by default — I like taking things apart to understand them, and I welcome constructive criticism and open discussion as a normal part of getting a design right, not a threat to it. That same curiosity is pulling me toward integrating AI with hardware through embedded systems, and toward automation more broadly: I think most repetitive, predictable tasks are candidates for automating away, so that people can spend their attention on the work that actually needs a person.

Alongside practical engineering work, I value good documentation, concise communication, and the shared habits that let teams move with confidence. Whether the work is a PCB, a deployed service, or an asset register, I try to make the next decision easier for the people who inherit it.

How I work

Five habits I bring to technical work.

01

Start with the operating condition.

A solution is only useful when it accounts for the environment, the information that is available, and the failure modes that matter.

02

Keep the control path legible.

Priorities, thresholds, fallback states, and data flows should be explainable—not hidden inside a pile of implementation details.

03

Connect the physical and digital layers.

Sensors, microcontrollers, interfaces, dashboards, and records all become stronger when each layer passes clear, usable information to the next.

04

Make collaboration an engineering feature.

Clear updates, documentation, and team accountability are part of the delivery—not an optional final step.

05

Automate the repeatable, not the judgment.

If a task is predictable and repetitive, it's usually worth automating — in firmware or in a daily workflow — so that time and attention go to the decisions that genuinely need a person.
